Determinants of Life Expectancy Across U.S. Counties

This project examines the relationship between health conditions, healthcare access, and county-level life expectancy across the United States. Using pooled county-year data from 2019–2023, the analysis applies multiple linear regression and logistic regression with Lasso-based variable selection to identify which health and insurance factors most strongly predict life expectancy outcomes.

This analysis was completed as part of a larger group research project, in which each member independently analyzed a different domain of predictors (socioeconomic, environmental, healthcare, mobility, and food systems) before the findings were synthesized into a comparative paper. This repository documents my individual contribution: the health conditions and healthcare access analysis, including my code, data processing, and findings.

Business Context

County of residence is often a stronger predictor of lifespan than genetics, and these geographic gaps in life expectancy have widened even as national health spending has climbed. Public health planners and policymakers need to understand which determinants carry the most weight once correlated factors are accounted for. This analysis focuses on whether disease burden and insurance coverage, independent of other socioeconomic and environmental factors, can reliably predict county-level life expectancy.

Dataset

  • CDC PLACES: Local Data for Better Health (2022, 2023, 2024 releases)
  • County Health Rankings, Life Expectancy Data (2019–2023)
  • U.S. Census Bureau, Healthcare Coverage, 2023 5-Year Estimates
  • 15,359 county-year observations across 5 years and 20 variables
  • Response variable: county-level life expectancy

Methods

  • Data merging and cleaning across three public health data sources
  • Exploratory data analysis and outlier screening
  • Variable selection via Lasso regression
  • Multiple linear regression with goodness-of-fit diagnostics
  • Logistic regression (binary above/below national average life expectancy)
  • Multicollinearity assessment via VIF
  • 10-fold cross-validation for model performance comparison

Final Results

  • Linear Regression: R² = 0.616 | RMSE = 1.777 (test set)
  • Logistic Regression: Accuracy = 81.43% | AUC = 0.897 (test set)
  • Both models confirmed stable via 10-fold cross-validation
  • Key predictors: Stroke and COPD prevalence were the strongest drivers of reduced life expectancy, consistent across both models
  • Lack of insurance coverage (Uninsured_Total) showed a meaningful negative effect independent of disease burden
